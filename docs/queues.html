Queues and Background Jobs

Run Asynchronous Work Without Hiding Delivery Semantics

Rustee models jobs as typed, at-least-once deliveries. Providers acknowledge only after a successful handler, retry failures according to explicit policy, and route terminal failures to a DLQ.

Core Contract

  • rustee-jobs provides typed envelopes with a 1 MiB framework byte limit and 255-byte application idempotency keys, stable job names limited to 255 bytes with no whitespace or NUL bytes, immutable-after-startup registries, strict current-version decode, explicit old-version upcasters, validated retry budgets and ordered non-zero delays, fail-closed direct retry decisions for invalid policies, provider-observed delivery attempts, and content-free envelope, upcast, and provider diagnostics with trusted sources for application handling.
  • Every job handler must be idempotent. A job ID or application idempotency key must protect database or external effects from duplicate delivery.
  • Malformed envelopes and unknown job registrations bypass ordinary retry and enter a durable DLQ path. Registered handler failures use the configured retry policy.
  • Jobs are not Kafka events. The queue API preserves acknowledgement, retry, and DLQ semantics; topic, partition, offset, and replay belong to event streams.

Provider contracts are tested individually. No adapter promises exactly-once execution, hides broker configuration, or turns delivery attempts into a universal queue semantic.

Outbox, Delays, and Scheduling

rustee-outbox-sqlx stages a job in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the business change. A destination-specific relay leases rows with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED and confirms only after the broker acknowledges the publish. Database and broker confirmation are not atomic, so duplicates remain possible.

  • JobSchedule::after and stage_job_after use the PostgreSQL clock for one-time delays of at least one millisecond. Due rows survive process restarts without an in-memory timer.
  • Outbox retry settlement treats an explicit zero delay as immediate eligibility; any non-zero delay must be at least one millisecond before PostgreSQL stores it.
  • OutboxPriority from 0 to 255 is local relay ordering only. It is not broker priority, fairness, or a global rate limit.
  • rustee-jobs-cron-sqlx registers typed recurring jobs with an explicit IANA time zone. DST gaps are skipped and repeated local time fires once at the earlier UTC occurrence. Each durable row revalidates the shared stable-job-name contract and its bounded JSON template before it materializes one fresh outbox job.
  • Its application-owned supervisor claims due schedules, stages a fresh job outbox row, and advances the next occurrence in one transaction. Late schedules stage at most once instead of replaying every missed tick.
  • An optional PostgreSQL-clock fixed-window governor uses a whole-millisecond window of at least one millisecond and may defer a schedule to the next boundary before it stages an outbox row. Application-owned schedule and shared-governor keys, plus durable schedule row identifiers, are redacted from framework diagnostics; storage code accesses them only through the explicit scheduler contract. An application can still choose to record a row identifier explicitly through Display. Holiday calendars, tenant fairness, external quotas, and catch-up policy remain application-owned.

Observability and Worker Operation

  • rustee-jobs-observability::JobMetrics and the optional Prometheus helper collect bounded provider, settlement outcome, and global duration data. They retain at most fifteen direct provider labels plus other, and create no registry, listener, or route automatically.
  • Never use job IDs, job types, payloads, queue routes, receipt handles, idempotency keys, trace carriers, or raw handler errors as metric labels. Trace correlation and export retention are application policy.
  • Workers can run separately from the HTTP server. Concurrency and prefetch must protect downstream databases and APIs. Shutdown stops intake even when every worker slot is occupied, then drains or aborts active work within the configured timeout. After a drain timeout, every provider reaps its aborted handler tasks before it returns. An aborted SQS delivery also stops and aborts its visibility heartbeat, so a cancelled handler cannot retain its lease in the background.
  • Readiness checks broker access, configured stream or queue state, and worker registration; it does not prove downstream effects are healthy.
  • Monitor DLQ backlog, retry rate, oldest pending age, processing latency, and provider-specific settlement signals. Outbox relays expose bounded pass outcomes and final drain reports.

DLQ Investigation and Replay

  1. Snapshot provider, observed attempt, source/DLQ route, discovery time, envelope checksum, and incident record. Keep payloads and receipt handles out of ordinary logs and metrics.
  2. For malformed or unknown jobs, repair the registry, schema, upcaster, or deployment configuration before considering replay.
  3. For a handler that may have run, determine the idempotency-ledger or external-provider outcome first. Do not replay an unknown external effect automatically.
  4. An approved replay decodes or explicitly upcasts to the current type, creates a new job envelope and job ID, and preserves the original application idempotency key where appropriate.
  5. Record the approver, scope, reason, stop condition, and reconciliation result. DLQ retention and deletion require an explicit policy.
